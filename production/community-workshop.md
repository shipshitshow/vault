# Community workshop — pilot proposal

Prepared October 2, 2026. Direction approved: a paid workshop with a concrete outcome. This is a reviewable offer proposal, not a scheduled event or an opened checkout. Demand, price and conversion are unvalidated.

## The offer

**Ship your first AI-assisted video workflow.** Bring a recording. Leave with one edited 60–90 second clip, a YouTube title and description, three thumbnail concepts, and a repeatable production folder you can use again.

For builders and small creators who use GPT or Claude but still handle recording, editing and packaging as disconnected chores. This narrower audience is a hypothesis to validate with viewers; the show's broader audience is builders choosing AI models and workflows.

The paid value is guided implementation, feedback and getting unstuck. The public skills, examples, transcripts and brand resources remain free. Participation does not buy exclusivity over those resources.

## Deliverable and readiness

- A playable clip exported from the participant's own short recording.
- A packaging file containing title candidates, final description, relevant tags and three distinct thumbnail directions.
- A channel profile plus a production folder with source, project, exports and metadata separated.
- A checklist for repeating the workflow, including reviewing factual claims and export quality.

Participants bring a 3–10 minute recording, an installed supported editor/plugin, and an existing GPT or Claude account. Tool subscriptions or usage are separate from the proposed ticket. Provide a small public sample recording for anyone who cannot bring material. Confirm the exact Tesseract setup and supported export workflow in a rehearsal before announcing dates or tickets.

Host rehearsal must produce the promised clip from a clean participant setup, within the workshop allocation. Prepare an editable sample project and exported fallback. Limit the first workshop to one editing path; do not promise support for every platform/model combination.

## Two-hour session

| Minutes | Work | Visible result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–10 | Choose the audience and one useful moment | One-sentence clip promise |
| 10–25 | Set up channel profile and folders | Reusable workspace |
| 25–45 | Identify claims, sources and the clip boundary | Selected segment with evidence |
| 45–80 | Edit with Tesseract and GPT/Claude | Playable draft; export fallback available |
| 80–100 | Package for YouTube | Title, description, tags and three thumbnail concepts |
| 100–110 | Watch and review | Export and factual quality checks |
| 110–120 | Show results and record next steps | Repeatable checklist and unfinished-work notes |

Vincent facilitates the workflow; confirm Mitchell's availability before promising a second host. Keep a small enough cohort for direct feedback. Offer a follow-up group review within seven days only if its time is included in the operating budget.

## Price and delivery hypothesis

Proposed pilot: **€79 per seat, 12-seat cap, five-seat minimum**. These numbers are an experiment, not evidence of willingness to pay. At 5–12 seats, hypothetical gross ticket revenue is €395–€948 before fees, taxes, refunds, software usage and host time. Revenue is not profit.

Measure preparation, delivery and follow-up hours to determine whether a repeatable workshop is viable. Do not open sales until the price, dates, tool requirements, delivery terms, cancellation/refund terms and minimum-cohort handling are approved. No payment provider or subscription has been provisioned for this proposal.

## Validation before launch

Use the next show's related workflow as a demonstration of the actual outcome. Draft one clear invitation and a simple interest form: desired outcome, current blocker, tool setup, availability and interest at the proposed price. Sending the invitation or collecting registrations is a separate action; it has not been performed.

Interview five interested viewers. Revise the promise if they want a different concrete workflow. A waitlist or a survey answer is not a purchase. Once the offer and delivery terms are approved, run a small presale; use actual paid seats as the demand signal.

Pilot success criteria are proposed: at least five paid seats, at least 80% of attending participants complete the clip/package, and the hosts can name the remaining blockers and time per participant. Track refunds and participant feedback. Repeat only after the delivery evidence supports it. A subscription community or paid toolkit can follow repeated demand; neither is the first build.
